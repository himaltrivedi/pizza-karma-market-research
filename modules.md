# Modules

Each module is a section of the report. Open with a one-sentence finding, then bullets and tables (not long prose), with an evidence label after every figure, written `value (Source, year, A/B/C)`. End each module with `Not found:` items, each with `Where to get it:`.

Core area = the principal city plus the core suburbs from `01-scope.md`. Fields are best effort: fill what you can find, mark the rest Not found. Report only what you found: no recommendations about entering the city, no "where the need is", no "where Pizza Karma fits". Pizza Karma's own locations are never competitors.

Event and demand files (from `pull_events.py`): `events_by_month.csv`, `venues_top.csv`, `sports_home_games.csv`, `airport_seasonality_index.csv`, `business_patterns_metro.csv`, `campus_enrollment.csv`, `k12_seniors.csv`, `city_open_data_candidates.csv`, and the agent's own `event_inventory.csv`.
Data pack files (in `data/`, A-level, cite file and year): `acs_metro.csv`, `acs_places_core.csv`, `acs_counties_*.csv` (population, density, income, age, % 18-34, Asian Indian counts, growth, margins of error), `noaa_monthly_normals.csv` (one named airport station), `schools_districts_metro.csv`, `colleges_metro.csv`, `metro_counties.csv`, `poi_counts_osm.csv` (OpenStreetMap counts of breweries, bars/pubs, restaurants, Indian restaurants, vegetarian/vegan restaurants, colleges and Hindu temples in a circle around each core place), and a city license file when one exists. Use them before searching. The OSM counts use the same 5 km circle around each place's center, so places can be compared with each other, but they include neighboring areas and OSM undercounts in some places (a known temple or restaurant may be missing): cite them as `(poi_counts_osm.csv, OpenStreetMap, year, B)`, use them only as relative proxies, cross-check the obvious cases with web research, and mark scores built on them low confidence.

## Module 8: Licensing, permits and operating rules (Tier 1, do first)
Goal: a complete checklist of what it takes to operate legally with a restaurant base and mobile trucks, with first-year fees. Name the issuing agency, the jurisdiction (city, county or state) and the official page for every item, and add a "confirm with the agency" note. Never present a regulation as final. Verify each fee and rule on the official page (A) before writing it, with a short verbatim quote. City codes are often only on code hosts (library.municode.com, codelibrary.amlegal.com, ecode360.com): if one is blocked, look for the ordinance on the city's own site, or give the chapter number under Where to get it. A fee schedule more than two years old is flagged with its date, not dropped.
- **Jurisdiction map.** State, counties and municipalities in the metro. Research the principal city and its county in full; then the county and municipality rules for the 3 largest suburbs in the core set, fully; every other suburb gets one line (own license or follows county, and the clerk's phone number) so none is left silently unread. Module 2's ranking comes later, so pick those 5 up front from `acs_places_core.csv` (largest population, plus Asian Indian share), and after the ranking is done research any top-5 suburb you missed. List every other county and suburb as "secondary, not researched". For each researched one: own mobile food vending license, or follows county/state; agency, license name, link. Note municipalities that ban or restrict trucks and whether a license in one jurisdiction is honored in another.
- **Restaurant base.** Entity and business registration, local business license, food establishment license and health plan review, zoning and certificate of occupancy, building and fire inspections, food manager and handler certification, signage, sales tax registration. If a fee depends on square footage or risk level, give the published formula or table and link it.
- **Mobile trucks.** Mobile food unit license and truck health inspection; whether a licensed restaurant can be the truck's base of operations (commissary rule) and what documentation that needs; fire rules for propane and generators (fire marshal permit, extinguishers, hood suppression, cylinder storage, generator limits); **tandoor / solid-fuel / open-flame approval** (state the open question plainly if unresolved); vehicle registration and commercial vehicle rules; parking, vending zone, distance-from-restaurant and time limits; noise and generator hours; private-property rules; temporary event and special-event vendor permits; insurance minimums (coverage level, not premium) from the city or typical event organizers.
- **Fee table.** For every item: agency, first-year fee as published (application, plan review, inspection, license), **one-time or annual**, date of the fee schedule, processing time, source URL, verbatim quote. List the fee for every permit even if annual. Subtotal only the fixed first-year fees and say exactly what the subtotal excludes; for a metro spanning many counties build it for the principal city's county and the most likely health risk type, and say which; list variable or per-event fees separately. No renewals after year one, no operating costs. A figure with no quote is B at best.
- **Launch sequence.** Numbered order of what must happen (entity registration, plan review, build-out, inspections, truck licensing) with typical lead times.
- **Confirm with agencies.** Anything the official pages leave unclear goes into the call sheet (`call_sheet.py add`: office, phone or email, the question, the rule it cites, why it matters; priority 1 = the tandoor / solid-fuel / open-flame approval). Paste the output of `call_sheet.py report` at the end of this module, and make the call sheet's first item the first line of the brief's risks.
- Pizza Karma's existing Twin Cities permits are not the target: only the chosen city's rules.

Report the table as a reading of names (not menus), say what share was classified, and look up the unclassified names that could be pizza or Indian before you say there are none.
- **Category breakdown** by cuisine: pizza, Indian/South Asian, burgers/American, tacos/Mexican, BBQ, East and Southeast Asian, Mediterranean/Middle Eastern, desserts/coffee, other. Count and share of each, 2-3 example names.
- **Vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free trucks:** how many center on them.

Up to 8 pizza and up to 5 Indian/South Asian vendors, or all if fewer (if few, say which sources you searched: a short list may reflect blocked pages), including any tandoor-fired or Indian-fusion pizza. For each: where and when it operates; signature items, menu style, dietary options; how it takes bookings; price for a main item, appetizer and drink (low to high, say how many menus you sampled; do not paste menus; best effort); social following, rating and review count with the platform named (best effort); sentiment themes in your own words (top praise, top complaints), never copied review text.
- **Fill every vendor's price, rating and review count by opening pages, not by trusting search summaries.** A WebSearch summary almost never carries them. For each vendor, in this order: (1) its own website or menu page; (2) its marketplace listing (Zola, WeddingWire, The Knot, Best Food Trucks, GigSalad, Thumbtack, ezCater, Roaming Hunger); (3) a search such as `"<vendor> <city> Yelp rating reviews"`, since aggregators (Mapquest, Uber Eats, DoorDash, The Infatuation) often show a rating and review count. Open each with WebFetch and ask for the exact lines: price per person or per item, rating, review count, menu items with prices, oven type, how to book. Record every fetch right away with `competitors.py add` (repeat adds for a vendor merge into one row, so add as you learn). If a page says "request pricing", use its FAQ average or a caterer's per-tray or per-person price and say so in `--price-item`; a price older than two years carries its year in `--price-item`. On HTTP 403 or 429, try the vendor's other listing before giving up. Do not leave a vendor at "Not found" until three sources are tried; then say which. Ratings from different platforms are never averaged together. Reviews on wedding marketplaces skew positive: say so.
- **Find more vendors than the first search gives.** Aim for at least 6 pizza vendors and 4 Indian/South Asian vendors with prices or a stated reason. For Indian/South Asian also check Indian restaurants and caterers that run a truck or cater events, halal and Pakistani-named licenses, and Roaming Hunger's Indian filter. Say how many you checked.
- **Price ranges** across each group.
- **Recurring praise and complaints.**
- **Common locations and booking channels.**
- Claims no stronger than the source ("advertises tandoori grills" does not show a tandoor fits on a truck).

## Module 3: Events and seasons (Tier 1)
Goal: the major recurring events and event categories by month, with enough detail (crowd size, food-truck policy, South Asian relevance) to choose what to pursue. Not individual bookings, applications or organizer contacts.
**Data first.** `pull_events.py` (workflow step 2) gives: `events_by_month.csv` and `venues_top.csv` (Ticketmaster: ticketed concerts, sports, theater and family events within 35 miles for the next 12 months; only when a key exists), `sports_home_games.csv` (MLB), `city_open_data_candidates.csv` (the city's own events, markets and permit datasets: open a useful one with `pull_events.py CITY --dataset DOMAIN ID`), `airport_seasonality_index.csv`. These cover ticketed events only: free festivals, markets, cultural and community events must come from research. City open data is A; Ticketmaster listings are B.
**Major events first (before the month-by-month coverage).** The owner wants the biggest crowds, so find them for the whole metro before the long tail:
- The principal city's 10 to 15 largest annual events by attendance, any category (festivals, parades, marathons and races, air shows, fairs, big concert series, holiday markets).
- For every core suburb, its 1 to 3 largest annual events (village festival, Labor Day or Fourth of July event, county fair, cultural mela), searching `"largest annual festival <suburb>"` and `"<suburb> events attendance"`, and the metro's big suburban venues (arenas, amphitheaters, convention centers).
- The largest South Asian events anywhere in the metro, not only the city.
For each: next published date (else the typical month), attendance (an organizer or listing claim is C and says so; a news report or city figure is B; an official page is A), the venue or suburb in `--venue` (the coverage check matches core suburb names there), whether trucks are accepted and the vendor application page URL if one exists. Use a month range (`--month "May-Oct"`) for a series instead of one row per month. Do not record booth, truck-space or vendor fees: those are costs, not permit fees. `event_inventory.py coverage` reports how many 5k+ events are listed and which core places still have none; keep going until both are fine or two searches in a row add nothing.

**Research in passes**, recording every event with `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/event_inventory.py add` (name, category, month, venue, size tier, truck rule, South Asian y/n, source, confidence):
- A. Seed from the pulled files: recurring series in `venues_top.csv`, the city datasets.
- B. Run `event_inventory.py coverage`. It lists months with fewer than 3 recurring events and categories with none. Search for those (one search per thin month, one per missing category) until coverage is good or two searches in a row add nothing.
- C. South Asian and cultural calendar: Diwali, Holi, Navratri/Garba, India Independence Day events, temple festivals, melas. Lunar dates shift: give the typical month.
- D. For the top 15 events, find crowd size (published attendance, or a tier: <1k, 1-5k, 5-25k, 25k+, est. with the reason) and whether food trucks are allowed (vendor page); `unknown` is allowed.
Then `event_inventory.py report` prints the monthly calendar, the category table and a scored top 10: edit that into the section instead of retyping it.
Rules: next published date, else the typical month with "next date not yet published"; never a past date as upcoming; list 8 to 12 sources to monitor, each with a link. Crowd-size tiers are `est.` with a `method:` line.

## Module 2: Population, neighborhoods and liveliness (Tier 2)
Goal: who lives where, and a scored ranking of areas. Do the ranking last in this module, after Modules 3, 5 and 7.
- **Demographics.** One table for the core set from `acs_places_core.csv` and `acs_metro.csv` (A): population, density, median household income, median age, % age 18-34, growth (note: `change_vs_2020_pct` compares the ACS 2020-24 average with the April 2020 census count; call it "ACS 2020-24 versus the 2020 census", not growth, and do not explain a difference as "estimate revisions" unless the 2020 census count shows it). Use `pop_change_pct` (two non-overlapping ACS periods) only when `pop_change_note` is empty or says "probably real"; where it says CHECK, use `change_vs_2020_pct` (ACS now vs the 2020 census) and say which you used. State the ACS year and that small places have large margins of error (use the `*_moe` columns). If the data pack is missing (no Census key), use Census QuickFacts/data.census.gov or the regional planning agency, else Not found.
- **South Asian population.** Asian Indian population and share by suburb, top concentrations; community anchors: temples, Indian grocery stores, cultural associations, community centers.
- **Dietary demand proxies.** Vegetarian/vegan and Indian restaurant counts by area from `poi_counts_osm.csv` (plus health-food stores from web research), labeled as proxies, not measurements.
- **Scoring rubric (use it so runs are comparable).** For numeric signals score each core place by quintile within the core set: 5 = top fifth, 4, 3, 2, 1 = bottom fifth. Signal 1 uses population and median income (average the two quintiles); signal 2 uses brewery + bar/pub counts from `poi_counts_osm.csv` plus any recurring events you found; signal 3 uses colleges, % age 18-34 and new apartments; signal 4 uses restaurant and bar counts and named dining/entertainment districts. A 5 needs the number behind it cited (the file and value) or a named source. Ties: use the average rank, score = round up of rank_fraction x 5. If a component needs a module you could not finish, average the components you have and say which are missing. A place with no OSM row (the step failed for it, even after the retry) is shown as n/a on the OSM-based signals; its total uses only the signals it has and it is listed below the fully scored places with a note, so a neutral guess never decides a rank. If an OSM count contradicts something you can verify (a known temple, a brewery district), use the verified fact, say so, and keep the OSM figure in the table for transparency. OSM undercounts some suburbs badly: do not let it alone rank a place last. State the rubric above the table.
- **Liveliness ranking.** Always scored. Score every core area 1-5 on four signals, weighted equally unless stated: (1) population and income; (2) event and brewery concentration; (3) student and young-professional presence (campuses, % 18-34, new apartments); (4) foot traffic and nightlife. Missing data is scored from proxies and marked "low confidence", never left blank. Use `poi_counts_osm.csv` for signals 2 and 4 (breweries, bars and pubs, restaurants per area) and signal 3 (colleges); say they are OpenStreetMap proxies and mark them low confidence. If that file is missing, use web-research proxies and say so. Show the scoring table with component scores, weights and total; rank the top 10 of the core set (the pull is by municipality, not neighborhood; add neighborhoods only where you have a source); explain each of the top 5 in one sentence; state how every score was derived. A score of 5 needs cited evidence. Fixed tie-break: higher population density, then higher median income, then alphabetical.
- **Neighborhoods of the principal city** (the owner operates inside the city, not only in suburbs): after the suburb pass, `neighborhood_counts_osm.csv` (from `pull_data.py "$ARGUMENTS" --neighborhoods-only`, run in the background) gives bar, brewery, restaurant and college counts around the city's named neighborhoods. `event_inventory.py neighborhoods` prints the scored top 10 with its method; put it in the section and in the brief's top 10 (neighborhoods and suburbs may be listed together, each marked). Add named districts the owner would expect (Loop, Devon Avenue...) by rerunning with `--neighborhood-names "A,B"`. It has no population signal; say so.
- Do not invent suburb figures outside the data pack. Breweries, entertainment districts and lunch centers come from Module 7.
